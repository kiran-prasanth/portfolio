Team Input
Went Well
Daily blocker check-ins caught the QA environment issue on Day 3 — gave us time to resolve before it cascaded.
Client review session on Day 6 was well-prepared. No revision requests. Signed off same day.
Dependency mapping at sprint planning was thorough — design-to-dev handoff had zero back-and-forth this sprint.
RAID log was actually used this sprint, not just filled in. Team flagged two risks proactively.
Did Not Go Well
Two tickets were estimated without full spec clarity. Both ballooned and caused the 6-point carry-over.
QA staging environment was not ready at sprint start. QA engineer had no work for 1.5 days.
Figma file versioning caused two designers to work on conflicting screen versions for half a day.
Meeting to review prototype ran 45 minutes over — no time limit set upfront.
Try Next Sprint
Do not pull a ticket into sprint planning unless the spec is complete and reviewed by the lead.
Dev lead confirms staging environment is live on Day 1 of sprint before QA is scheduled to start.
All review meetings have a time limit set at the start. Facilitator owns the clock.
Single source Figma file per project — no branches without PM and design lead approval.

Carry-over Root Cause

The 6 carry-over points came from two tickets that entered the sprint without complete specs. Both expanded during development when edge cases surfaced that were not accounted for at estimation. The spec-ready gate action item directly addresses this. No carry-over caused a client deadline impact — buffer within the sprint absorbed it.
