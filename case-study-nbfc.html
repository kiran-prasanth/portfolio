Context

The client was a non-banking financial company running a consumer lending product. Their existing loan application flow had accumulated redundant steps over several product iterations, creating friction at the point of disbursement that was measurably increasing drop-off rates and extending average completion time for applicants.

GoProtoz was engaged to rebuild the end-to-end application experience, from eligibility check through disbursement confirmation, across 50+ screens covering both the applicant-facing flow and the loan officer view.

My Role

Project Manager. I owned delivery from initial discovery briefing through final QA sign-off and developer handoff. The team included 2 UX researchers, 3 UI designers, and 1 QA engineer. I ran all sprint ceremonies, owned the Jira board, managed client status communication, and tracked every open item to closure.

Delivery Timeline
Week 1–2
Discovery and researchStructured user interviews with loan applicants and loan officers. Research outputs (journey maps, pain point synthesis) used to scope the redesign brief and inform sprint 1 priorities.
Week 3–6
Design: applicant flowSprint 1 and 2 covered the applicant-facing screens from eligibility to document upload. All screens reviewed by client PM at mid-sprint and end-of-sprint checkpoints. Three revision cycles managed without timeline impact.
Week 7–9
Design: loan officer view and disbursementDuring sprint 3, research surfaced a redundant step in the disbursement confirmation screen. The step was eliminated, the flow was resequenced, and the updated screens were delivered within the same sprint without moving the deadline.
Week 10–11
QA and handoffQA testing against acceptance criteria for all 50+ screens. Developer handoff package prepared: annotated designs, component specs, interaction notes, and edge case documentation. Signed off by client before final delivery.
Key Challenges
  • Client stakeholders had conflicting opinions on the disbursement flow. Managed this by presenting research findings in a structured session, agreeing on a single direction, and locking scope before sprint 3 began.
  • Mid-project the client requested addition of a loan officer escalation screen not in the original scope. Assessed impact, negotiated the addition against a lower-priority screen, updated the sprint plan, and kept the final deadline intact.
  • QA cycle was initially underscoped. Flagged this in sprint 3 planning, negotiated a buffer with the client, and restructured the final sprint to include QA time without requiring an additional sprint.
Outcome

Delivered on time with no deadline extensions. The redundant disbursement step, once identified and removed, reduced the average loan-completion time by 15% according to post-launch metrics shared by the client. The engagement converted into a retainer for a follow-on project.
